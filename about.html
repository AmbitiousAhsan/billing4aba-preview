Why Billing4ABA exists

I kept watching good ABA owners lose money to billing chaos.

I'm Ahsan. I've been doing ABA billing for years — alongside a team with 50+ combined years in ABA claims, denials, credentialing, and AR — and I kept seeing the same painful pattern. Brilliant BCBAs, the kind of clinicians families wait months to see, drowning in denials, credentialing dead-ends, and aging AR they didn't have time to chase.

Most of them had tried a generic biller at some point. The story was almost always the same: their account was one of a hundred across pediatrics, mental health, and ABA, and ABA — with its authorizations, units, modifiers, and payer quirks — was the one nobody on the other end actually understood.

So we stopped working around that and built the thing that should have existed all along: a billing partner that only does ABA, runs it like an operator, and stays small enough to actually know your clinic.

Billing4ABA itself is new — intentionally ABA-only, founder-led, and taking on a small number of first clients. The experience behind it isn't new: the team has spent years inside ABA revenue-cycle work — claims, denials, credentialing, authorizations, AR — across Texas Medicaid and commercial payers.

We're selective about who we take on. The goal is not to serve every clinic — it's to work with clinics where we can understand the billing mess clearly, build the right workflow, and communicate honestly.

What I've actually handled.

These are the specific payers, CPT codes, and modifiers I actively manage for current ABA practices — not theory. The specific payers, codes, and workflows I run day-to-day.

Payer mix

Texas Medicaid / TMHP (Superior, Aetna Better Health, Community First), BCBS TX, Aetna, Cigna, UnitedHealthcare, Tricare, Humana — Medicaid and commercial side by side.

Claims & denials

97151–97158 coding, payer-specific modifiers (HM/HO/HN) and POS logic, root-cause denial work, appeals with clinical and coding language.

Credentialing

LLC / EIN / Type 2 NPI guidance, CAQH setup + attestations, payer applications, re-credentialing before expiry, live status tracking.

Authorizations

Initial and concurrent auth requests, unit-used vs unit-approved tracking per client, reauth flagged 30+ days before expiry — we work to prevent lapses.

Reporting

Weekly AR aging (0–30, 31–60, 61–90+), denial-reason breakdown, collections vs. expected, plain-English summaries — not a black-box dashboard.

Compliance

BAA (Business Associate Agreement) terms signed before any PHI access. HIPAA-trained team, MFA on every account, least-privilege access, encryption in transit and at rest. Detailed audit logs for every PHI touchpoint, plus breach notification procedures in place so you know exactly what happens if something ever goes wrong.
